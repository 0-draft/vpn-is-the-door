## 92 answers named a route

Every number on this page comes from one question in NPA's survey of ransomware victims: the route the intruder used to get in (感染経路). NPA counts only answers that named a route; "unknown" and blank answers are left out.

In 2025 NPA received {C1.reports} ransomware reports, and {C1.n} answers named a route. The shares on this page are shares of those {C1.n}.

## The VPN door: 61 of 92

<Fig lang="en" n="1-2" caption="Minato Precision head office. The VPN door and its 61 cases; the other openings greyed" sources={["npa-r7-data"]} composite nts wide>
  <OpeningPlan lang="en" focus="vpn" uid="c1-vpn" label="Floor plan with the VPN door highlighted: 61 of 92 cases in 2025" />
  <RouteTable slot="data" lang="en" focus={["vpn"]} />
</Fig>

A VPN appliance lets staff, branch offices and maintenance contractors connect to the company network from outside. It sits at the internet edge, often as a function of the firewall.

Its login page answers anyone on the internet, and until someone logs in it cannot tell an employee from a stranger. A flaw in the code that runs before login can be used by anyone who finds the device.

On the plan it is a door in the outer wall that opens into the corridor behind reception. In 2025 it was the route in {C1.vpn} of {C1.n} cases ({C1.vpnPct}%). [Chapter 3](../the-doors/) covers the products and their flaws.

## The window: remote desktop, 19

<Fig lang="en" n="1-3" caption="Minato Precision head office. The remote desktop window and its 19 cases" sources={["npa-r7-data"]} composite nts wide>
  <OpeningPlan lang="en" focus="rdp" uid="c1-rdp" label="Floor plan with the window highlighted: 19 of 92 cases in 2025" />
  <RouteTable slot="data" lang="en" focus={["rdp"]} />
</Fig>

Remote desktop lets a user work on another computer's screen from a distance; on Windows it is the Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP). IT staff and maintenance contractors use it to manage servers.

It is meant for the internal network. It becomes reachable from the internet when a router forwards the connection straight to a server, for example so that a contractor can connect without a VPN. NPA's 2023 report describes these cases as remote desktop services set up with weak credentials (「強度の弱い認証情報等が設定されたリモートデスクトップサービス」).

On the plan it is a window that opens straight into one room. In 2025 it was the route in {C1.rdp} of {C1.n} cases ({C1.rdpPct}%). The highest year was 2024, with {ROUTE_YEARS[3]?.counts.rdp} of {ROUTE_YEARS[3]?.n}.

Anyone can send a message to a company address, so mail passes through filtering before it reaches staff, and the PCs it lands on run antivirus. On the plan, these checks are the reception desk at the main entrance.

NPA's label is 「不審メールやその添付ファイル」, suspicious email or its attachments. In 2025 it was the route in {C1.mail} of {C1.n} cases ({C1.mailPct}%).

## Outside Japan, edge devices became a main target

Verizon's Data Breach Investigations Report (DBIR) counts breaches worldwide. Its dataset and categories differ from NPA's, so the percentages cannot be compared.

## What the NPA tables can answer

The {C1.vpn} VPN answers for 2025 mix at least two techniques that ATT&CK keeps apart: logging in with valid credentials through an external remote service ([T1133](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1133/), [T1078](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1078/)), and exploiting a flaw in the internet-facing appliance ([T1190](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1190/)). Multi-factor authentication (MFA) stops a reused password but is never reached by a flaw that works before login.

In NPA's table, such a case is one VPN answer on a patched device. JPCERT/CC advises checking for signs of intrusion before updating the firmware and, if intrusion cannot be ruled out, considering a change of the passwords of the accounts in use.
