  <p>The National Police Agency (NPA) surveys ransomware victims and asks how the intruder got in. In 2025, {C1.n} answers named a route. {C1.vpn} named the VPN appliance and {C1.rdp} named remote desktop. Suspicious email accounted for {C1.mail}.</p>

## <Scale s="1:500" /> 92 answers named a route

Every number on this page comes from one question in NPA's survey of ransomware victims: the route the intruder used to get in (感染経路). NPA counts only the answers that named a route. Answers of "unknown" and unanswered questions are left out.

In 2025 NPA received {C1.reports} ransomware reports, and {C1.n} survey answers named a route. The shares on this page are shares of those {C1.n}, not of all {C1.reports}.

## <Scale s="1:500" /> The VPN door: 61 of 92

<Fig lang="en" n="1-2" caption="Minato Precision head office. The VPN door and its 61 cases; the other openings greyed" sources={["npa-r7-data"]} composite nts wide>
  <OpeningPlan lang="en" focus="vpn" uid="c1-vpn" label="Floor plan with the VPN door highlighted: 61 of 92 cases in 2025" />
  <RouteTable slot="data" lang="en" focus={["vpn"]} />
</Fig>

A VPN appliance lets staff, branch offices and maintenance contractors connect to the company network from outside, as if they were at their desks. It usually sits at the internet edge, often as a function of the firewall.

A person working from home has to reach its login page from any connection, so the login page answers anyone on the internet. Until someone logs in, the appliance cannot tell an employee from a stranger. A flaw in the code that runs before login can be used by anyone who finds the device.

On the plan it is a door in the outer wall that opens into the corridor behind reception. Whoever comes in this way never passes the reception desk. In 2025 it was the route in {C1.vpn} of {C1.n} cases ({C1.vpnPct}%). [Chapter 3](../the-doors/) covers the products and their flaws.

## <Scale s="1:500" /> The window: remote desktop, 19

<Fig lang="en" n="1-3" caption="Minato Precision head office. The remote desktop window and its 19 cases" sources={["npa-r7-data"]} composite nts wide>
  <OpeningPlan lang="en" focus="rdp" uid="c1-rdp" label="Floor plan with the window highlighted: 19 of 92 cases in 2025" />
  <RouteTable slot="data" lang="en" focus={["rdp"]} />
</Fig>

Remote desktop shows one computer's screen on another and lets the user work on it from there. In a company it is how IT staff and maintenance contractors manage servers without walking to the server room. On Windows it is the Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP).

It is meant for the internal network. It becomes reachable from the internet when a router forwards the connection straight to a server, for example so that a contractor can connect without a VPN. The only thing between the internet and that server is then a login screen. NPA's 2023 report describes these cases as remote desktop services set up with weak credentials (「強度の弱い認証情報等が設定されたリモートデスクトップサービス」).

On the plan it is a window: visible from the street, and it opens into one room instead of the corridor. In 2025 it was the route in {C1.rdp} of {C1.n} cases ({C1.rdpPct}%). The highest year was 2024, with {ROUTE_YEARS[3]?.counts.rdp} of {ROUTE_YEARS[3]?.n}.

## <Scale s="1:500" /> The main entrance: email, 2

<Fig lang="en" n="1-5" caption="Minato Precision head office. The main entrance, where email arrives: 2 cases" sources={["npa-r7-data"]} composite nts wide>

Email reaches every employee, and anyone on the internet can send a message to a company address. An address is public by design. For that reason mail passes through filtering before it reaches anyone, and the PCs it lands on run antivirus. On the plan, these checks are the reception desk at the main entrance.

NPA's label is 「不審メールやその添付ファイル」, suspicious email or its attachments. In 2025 it was the route in {C1.mail} of {C1.n} cases ({C1.mailPct}%).

Both were set up so that someone outside could work on the inside, for staff working from home or a contractor maintaining a server. To do that job, each has to accept connections from the internet before it knows who is connecting. Each decides on entry with a login check, or with the code that runs before the login check. Neither route passes the mail filtering at the main entrance.

For an IT manager, this is where the inventory starts: every device and service that accepts a login from the internet, who patches it, and whether it is still needed.

In the 2025 edition, edge devices and VPNs were 22% of the targets of vulnerability exploitation, almost eight times the 3% of the year before. Half of those flaws took longer than that to close.

## <Scale s="1:5" /> The NPA count records the opening, not the technique

The {C1.vpn} VPN answers for 2025 mix at least two techniques that ATT&CK keeps apart: logging in with valid credentials through an external remote service ([T1133](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1133/), [T1078](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1078/)), and exploiting a flaw in the internet-facing appliance itself ([T1190](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1190/)). The defenses differ. MFA stops a reused password but is never reached by a flaw that works before login.

The patched-but-entered cases show why the split matters. In NPA's table, such a case is one VPN answer on a patched device. JPCERT/CC advises checking for intrusion before updating the firmware and changing the passwords of the accounts in use.
