## What leadership decides

Exploitation of a published flaw starts within days; measured fixes take a month or more. Leadership sets the patch deadline. Name an owner, and provide a standby unit or HA pair so a reboot does not wait for sign-off.

## What the IT team does

Most of these items cost staff time more than money.

In the remaining cases the device was patched and the intruder still got in. NPA's data does not say how. JPCERT/CC documents one way: a password copied before the patch.

After a VPN KEV: preserve, then patch or rebuild, then reset credentials, then hunt behind the device. Patching first destroys the logs that show whether a reset and a hunt are needed.

## Each control stops the route at one point

On the plan from [chapter 2](../after-the-door/), choose how the intruder gets in, then switch on controls.

The scenarios are fixed. The stolen password belongs to a VPN account nobody uses any more. The pre-auth flaw is first exploited on day 5 after the fix is published, close to Mandiant's 2023 average. The zero-day is exploited before any fix exists, as JPCERT/CC reported for CVE-2025-0282 in Japan.
