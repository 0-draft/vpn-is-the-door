<Summary lang="en">
  <p>Every control works at one point on the intruder's route. A patch does nothing against a flaw exploited before the fix existed, multi-factor authentication (MFA) nothing against a flaw reached before login. An offline backup undoes the encryption but not the leak.</p>
  <p>Leadership decides three things: an owner for every internet-facing device, how many days a fix may take, and the budget for a backup the intruder cannot reach. [Fig. 6-5](#fig-6-5) lets you switch each control on and see where the route stops.</p>

## What leadership decides

Exploitation of a published flaw starts within days; measured fixes take a month or more. Leadership sets the patch deadline. Name an owner, and provide a standby unit or HA pair so a reboot does not wait for sign-off.

## What the IT team does

Most of these items cost staff time more than money.

<Fig lang="en" n="6-3" caption="Work for the IT team, with the reason for each and who owns it" sources={["osaka-report-2023", "cisa-eos-edge-2026", "cisa-kev", "npa-r8h1-data", "jpcert-ir-2024q2", "cisa-aa24-109a", "handa-report-main"]}>
  <div class="table-scroll" tabindex="0">
    <table>
      <thead><tr><th scope="col">Task</th><th scope="col">Why</th><th scope="col">Owner</th></tr></thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr><td>Keep a list of every internet-facing device: model, firmware version, end-of-support date, owner, maintaining vendor.</td><td>Osaka had no asset inventory. CISA, FBI and NCSC ask for one covering edge devices and their support timelines.</td><td>IT team. Vendors supply the data for their devices.</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Check the CISA KEV catalog and vendor advisories every working day; patch to the deadline.</td><td>KEV lists flaws already used in attacks. In NPA's data for 2022 to 2025, the entry device was missing patches in {HEADLINE.unpatched.yes} of {HEADLINE.unpatched.n} cases.</td><td>IT team.</td></tr>
        <tr><td>After any flaw in the VPN appliance, change every password it held and end all sessions, even if it is patched.</td><td>JPCERT/CC handled intrusions into patched SSL-VPN appliances with credentials stolen before the patch.</td><td>IT team. Vendors reset their own accounts.</td></tr>
        <tr><td>MFA on every VPN account. Delete unused accounts, contractors' included.</td><td>CISA's Akira advisory names VPN access without MFA as an entry point.</td><td>IT team.</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Separate admin accounts for administrators; a different local administrator password on every server.</td><td>At Osaka, one built-in Administrator password shared across servers made lateral movement easy.</td><td>IT team.</td></tr>
        <tr><td>One backup copy offline or immutable, under separate credentials; test a restore.</td><td>Osaka's critical-care backups were online only and could not be used. Offline tapes restored the rest. At Handa, offline backups up to 2018 survived; later data was recovered by the forensics vendor.</td><td>IT team.</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Before touching a suspect device, export its logs and configuration.</td><td>At both hospitals, the firmware upgrade erased the VPN logs.</td><td>IT team, with the vendor.</td></tr>
      </tbody>
    </table>
  </div>
</Fig>

In the remaining cases the device was patched and the intruder still got in. NPA's data does not say how. JPCERT/CC documents one way: a password copied before the patch.

After a VPN KEV: preserve, then patch or rebuild, then reset credentials, then hunt behind the device. Patching first destroys the logs that show whether a reset and a hunt are needed.

## Each control stops the route at one point

On the plan from [chapter 2](../after-the-door/), choose how the intruder gets in, then switch on controls.

The scenarios are fixed. The stolen password belongs to a VPN account nobody uses any more. The pre-auth flaw is first exploited on day 5 after the fix is published, close to Mandiant's 2023 average. The zero-day is exploited before any fix exists, as JPCERT/CC reported for CVE-2025-0282 in Japan.

With every control on and the zero-day chosen, the intruder still gets in, and data the first account can read still leaves.
