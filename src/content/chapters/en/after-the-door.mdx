## The logs each step leaves

Each step leaves a record in a log most companies already have: the VPN appliance, Active Directory, the file server, the backup software.

### Step 2: a login that does not fit the person

To the appliance, this is a normal login with a valid account.

<Signs lang="en" step={2} time="T+0">
  <ul>
    <li><span class="where">VPN log</span>A successful login at 03:00 by an account whose owner is asleep, from an address outside Japan or at a hosting provider.</li>
    <li><span class="where">VPN log</span>A run of failed logins just before it, or none at all if the password had leaked.</li>
    <li><span class="where">VPN log</span>A login on an account that should not exist any more: a former employee, or a contractor whose contract has ended.</li>
  </ul>
</Signs>

### Step 3: one remote worker touches dozens of machines

The intruder now has an inside address and starts mapping the network. Nothing passed through email filtering or antivirus, so they record nothing.

<Signs lang="en" step={3} time="within 5 minutes">
  <ul>
    <li><span class="where">firewall</span>One VPN session connecting to dozens of internal addresses within minutes.</li>
    <li><span class="where">servers</span>Remote desktop attempts to many servers from one machine. In the Osaka hospital case, information on about 240 addresses was collected and remote desktop tried to about 70 from one server.</li>
  </ul>
  <SourceLine lang="en" ids={["arcticwolf-akira-2025", "osaka-report-2023"]} />
</Signs>

Arctic Wolf saw Akira operators start scanning the internal network "within five minutes" of logging in to a SonicWall VPN.

### Step 4: a new administrator appears

The intruder takes over or creates an administrator account in Active Directory.

<Signs lang="en" step={4} time="median 3.40 h">
  <ul>
    <li><span class="where">AD</span>A user account nobody requested, or an existing account added to the Domain Admins group.</li>
    <li><span class="where">AD</span>An administrator account logging on from a VPN address.</li>
    <li><span class="where">servers</span>Antivirus removed or switched off on a server. In Osaka it was uninstalled from the first server the intruder reached.</li>
  </ul>
  <SourceLine lang="en" ids={["sophos-aar-2026", "osaka-report-2023"]} />
</Signs>

In Sophos's 2025 case data, the median time from gaining access to the first attempt on Active Directory was 3.40 hours.

### Step 5: backup jobs start failing

This usually happens in the last hours or days before encryption.

<Signs lang="en" step={5} time="before encryption">
  <ul>
    <li><span class="where">backup</span>Backup jobs fail, or the backup server stops answering.</li>
    <li><span class="where">backup</span>Someone logs in to the backup console at night.</li>
    <li><span class="where">servers</span>Shadow copies, the restore points Windows keeps on each server, disappear.</li>
  </ul>
</Signs>

### Step 6: one account reads everything

Data is usually copied out days into the attack.

<Signs lang="en" step={6} time="median 3 days">
  <ul>
    <li><span class="where">file server</span>One account reading thousands of files in a short time.</li>
    <li><span class="where">servers</span>Large compressed archives appearing on a server.</li>
    <li><span class="where">firewall</span>Outbound traffic far above normal, often to a cloud storage service.</li>
  </ul>
  <SourceLine lang="en" ids={["sophos-aar-2025", "sophos-aar-2026", "cisa-aa24-109a"]} />
</Signs>

CISA reports one Akira case that took "just over two hours". In Sophos's 2024 data the attack was detected a median of 2.7 hours after exfiltration.

### Step 7: every file changes its name at once

Encryption is the first step anyone notices without reading a log.

<Signs lang="en" step={7} time="under 4 h, sometimes 55 min">
  <ul>
    <li><span class="where">file server</span>Thousands of files renamed with a new extension within minutes. The Osaka files ended in <code>.elbie</code>.</li>
    <li><span class="where">printers</span>Ransom notes printing on every printer, as at Handa Hospital before dawn.</li>
    <li><span class="where">servers</span>Servers and virtual machines stopping.</li>
  </ul>
  <SourceLine lang="en" ids={["arcticwolf-akira-2025", "osaka-report-2023", "handa-report-main"]} />
</Signs>

## Time from entry to encryption

In the fastest cases, VPN login to encryption takes a few hours. The medians for data theft run to days. Each source starts its clock at a different point.

## Osaka General Medical Center, 2022

Osaka General Medical Center (大阪急性期・総合医療センター) was hit in October 2022, and its investigation committee published what happened, with page numbers. The report names the companies only by letter (E社, C社) and does not name the VPN product; it does name the vulnerability.

## ATT&CK techniques and the logs that show them

Technique IDs and names are from [MITRE ATT&CK](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/enterprise/). Most Security log events need the matching audit policy enabled. Appliance logs are lost in an upgrade unless they were sent off the box first.
