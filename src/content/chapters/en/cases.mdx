  <p>In every case here, the organization or an official investigation stated how the intruder got in. Nine of the fifteen name a VPN appliance. Five say only "network equipment", "communications equipment", "remote connection device" or "connection point with external networks", and this chapter keeps those words even where a government committee or a published article later pointed to a VPN. One names an outside contractor's account and not the device it logged in to.</p>
  <p>In the four hospital and port investigations, none of the devices' own logs were available (at Nagoya they had never been collected), and three of the four devices accepted connections from anywhere on the internet.</p>

## How the cases were chosen

A case is here only if the organization or an official report named the way in. Incidents where the route is known only from press reports, security researchers or the attackers' own claims are left out, even when often cited as VPN cases. Credentials quoted in the reports are not reproduced.

<CaseCounts lang="en" />

The case pages follow the Japan Transport Safety Board's accident reports, which state that an investigation is meant to prevent the next accident and not to assign blame.
